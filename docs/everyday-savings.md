# 🍱 Everyday savings (no birthday or signup needed)

Dull but compounding: the always-on patterns that knock ₩2,000–30,000 off daily food costs
without any preparation.

> Last verified: 2026-08-03

## 🏪 Convenience stores: the 1+1 / 2+1 system
This is the single biggest everyday food hack in Korea:
- CU, GS25, 7-Eleven and emart24 all run **monthly promo items** marked **1+1** (buy one get one) or **2+1** (buy two get one free) — drinks, ham, snacks, kimbap, lunch boxes.
- **No membership required** — just watch the shelf tags. Promos change on the 1st of each month.
- Official monthly promo lists: CU <https://cu.bgfretail.com>, GS25 <https://gs25.gsretail.com>, 7-Eleven <https://www.7-eleven.co.kr>, emart24 <https://www.emart24.co.kr> (each has an "행사/이벤트상품" page). 🟢 permanent fixtures of Korean retail, 🟡 individual items rotate monthly
- Store apps (PocketCU, 우리동네GS, 세븐일레븐) add app-only coupons and *stamp save* for coffee, but signup needs Korean phone verification (📱) — fine to skip as a tourist.
- Pro play: late-night discount stickers (심야 할인) on lunch boxes/sushi near closing time at some emart24/CU branches — ask staff which hour.

## ☕ Coffee strategy
- **Chain budget tier**: Mega Coffee, Compose Coffee, Backdabang offer large iced americanos at ₩1,500–2,500 (vs ₩4,700+ at Starbucks) with zero prep.
- Starbucks KR: paying with a registered KR card adds **1 free Extra** (shot/syrup) and the welcome coupon — see `signup-welcome-deals.md`.

## 🥡 Restaurant habits that save money
- Lunch special (런치특선): many Korean full-service restaurants run ~20–40% cheaper lunch sets, 11:00–14:00ish, no coupon needed — especially in Myeongdong/Jongno business districts.
- Unlimited-refill banchan (반찬) — free refill of side dishes is standard culture; cheap meals stretch far.
- Buffets at lunch: Ashley Queens weekday lunch is dramatically cheaper than dinner; VIPS weekday lunch ₩39,700 vs dinner/weekend ₩47,900–49,700 (Banksalad, out of official VIPS pages, Dec 2025).
- Water is always free; tipping is **never** part of the bill — mentally adjust "cheap vs. home country" math accordingly.
